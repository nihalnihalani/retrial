# Winning Playbook — Gary-Yau Chan principles mapped to Daytona HackSprint #5

Source: growthwithgary.com "How to win a hackathon" (26x winner). Mapped to OUR event: 5.5h build, 3-min demo, panel = 2 Daytona + 2 CodeRabbit + 2 CopilotKit + 1 Braintrust + 1 Fireworks engineers + TikTok eng + Lyzr AI founder.

## Judge-type analysis of OUR panel
- **8/10 are API-Evangelist/engineer judges** (sponsor staff). Gary: "Integrate their API in the most unique way. Optimize for UNUSED/UNPOPULAR functions — gamify them. They love creativity on non-core functions. Don't over-brief them beforehand; they prefer to be WOWed at the demo... but DO befriend them during the day to validate approach + get tagged as using their API."
- **CTO-type judges** (TikTok eng, sponsor engineers wearing CTO hats): show real code, expect edge-case/scalability questions, conventional economics. A shallow app + market slides will NOT impress.
- **Founder-type** (Lyzr AI): startup viability, "can I picture this as a company."
- Almost NO pure investor/BD judges → market-size slides get ~30 seconds max, not a pillar.

## Tactical rules for the day
1. **Befriend the evangelists at 9:00-10:00** — every sponsor booth visit: confirm our use of their newest feature, get taught the gotchas, get "tagged" as using their API. Do NOT reveal the full wow moment.
2. **Front-end first** — 1-2 hero features max, polished. Judges only see the front-end. Skip sign-in entirely; land on content. Loading animations while agents "think" (buys presenter recovery time + signals real computation).
3. **Codename now, never bikeshed naming.**
4. **Validation artifacts even in 5.5h**: poll other teams at lunch ("would you use a 4-model race to fix your bugs?"), screenshot the tally, photo with a "customer" team. 2 artifacts minimum for slides.
5. **Demo format**: Problem → Solution → DEMO (the fork moment) → Validation artifacts → Business model (one line) → Roadmap → Team creds. Passion, loud, NEVER say "we didn't have time."
6. **Interactive/skit element**: involve judges/audience (e.g. let a judge pick the bug to fix, or raise-hands question "who's waited 20 min for CI today?"). Memorable > polished.
7. **Q&A time maximization**: if no questions, keep selling — show extra features/validation slides. One teammate answers, another drives slides in background.
8. **Presentation order matters**: early slot = more questions (be ready); late slot = tired judges (be loud, be the wake-up).
9. **Team roles**: front-end owner is mandatory; a BD-voice presenter tells the story; engineers handle Q&A depth.
10. **Unspoken judge question**: "can I deem this team winners so they keep building it?" — end the pitch with genuine intent to continue.
